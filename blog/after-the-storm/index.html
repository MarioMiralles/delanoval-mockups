After the storm: what to document before you call your insurer

By Lazaro De La Noval 5 min read Updated October 2026

The hours and days after a storm are stressful, and the steps you take before you call your insurance company can shape how your claim is handled. Here is what to document, and why it matters.

Step 01Make sure everyone is safe first

No photo is worth an injury. Stay clear of downed power lines, standing water and damaged structures, and wait until local authorities say it is safe to return.

Step 02Photograph and film everything before cleanup

Before you move, repair or throw anything away, capture the damage in detail:

  • Wide shots of every side of the home, then close-ups of each damaged area.
  • The roof, gutters, windows and fences, photographed from the ground. Do not climb onto a damaged roof.
  • Inside: ceilings, walls and floors, and anything stained, warped or wet.
  • Damaged belongings, before you move them.
  • A slow video walk-through of the property, narrating what you see.

Your phone records the date and time of each photo automatically. Keep the original files and back them up.

Step 03Prevent further damage, and keep every receipt

Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to protect the property from further damage, such as covering a damaged roof with a tarp or removing standing water. Photograph the damage first, then make temporary repairs and keep receipts for every material and service.

Hold off on permanent repairs until the damage has been documented and inspected.

Step 04List what was damaged

Write down each damaged item, roughly when you bought it and what it cost. Receipts, bank statements and old photos all help. If something must be thrown away for health reasons, photograph it first and note it on your list.

Step 05Find your policy and declarations page

Your full policy and declarations page show what is covered, your deductibles (including any separate hurricane deductible) and the conditions you must follow after a loss. If you cannot find them, ask your insurer or agent for a certified copy.

Florida deadline

For most property claims, Florida law now requires notice to your insurer within one year of the date of loss. Do not wait to report.

Step 06Report the claim and keep a claim log

Report the claim promptly and write down your claim number. From then on, keep a simple log of every call, email and inspection: the date, the person you spoke with and what was said. Save every letter and email from the insurer.

Step 07Be careful before you sign or accept

An adjuster may ask for a recorded statement or offer a quick payment. Before you agree to anything, make sure you understand what you are signing and what you may be giving up.

If your claim is denied or delayed, or the offer seems low, an attorney can review your policy and the insurer's decision with you.
